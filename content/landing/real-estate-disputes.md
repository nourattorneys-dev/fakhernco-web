## Property disputes run to deadlines

Rental and property claims in the UAE are governed by strict procedures and short
windows — for notices, for filing, and for appeals. Missing one can decide the
matter regardless of who was right, and it is the single most common reason a
good case is lost.

If you have received a notice, the date on it matters more than almost anything
else in the file.

## Disputes we handle

- **Landlord and tenant** — rent increases, eviction notices, renewal disputes,
  security deposits, unpaid rent, early termination
- **Sale and purchase** — breach of the sale agreement, failure to complete,
  deposit and forfeiture disputes
- **Off-plan and developer claims** — delayed handover, failure to deliver,
  changes to the unit, the layout or the project
- **Snagging and construction defects** in a delivered property
- **Service charges** and owners' association disputes
- **Joint ownership**, co-owner disputes and partition
- **Inheritance** of UAE property

## Eviction: the notice is the case

Most eviction disputes are decided by whether the notice was valid — the ground
relied on, the period given, and the method of service. A landlord who serves
informally, or on the wrong ground, will often lose regardless of the merits. A
tenant who assumes a notice is valid because it looks official will often move
out when they did not have to.

Either way, the document is worth ten minutes of a lawyer's time before it is
worth a hearing.

## Off-plan and delayed handover

Delay claims turn on the sale and purchase agreement: what completion date it
committed to, what it says about extension, and what remedy it provides. Many
agreements are drafted to give the developer considerable latitude — but not
unlimited latitude, and not always as much as the developer asserts when a buyer
complains.

Bring the agreement and the payment schedule and we will tell you what you
actually have.

## Common questions

**I have received an eviction notice. How long do I have?**
That depends on the ground relied on and how the notice was served. Bring it in —
this is the most time-sensitive category of matter on this page.

**My developer has missed the handover date. Do I have a claim?**
Often, and it may include compensation. It depends on the terms of the sale
agreement and the length and cause of the delay.

**Can my landlord increase the rent by any amount?**
No. Increases are regulated and must follow the applicable rules and notice
periods. We will tell you whether the increase you have been given is lawful.

**The landlord is keeping my deposit. Is it worth pursuing?**
Sometimes it is not, and we will say so. We would rather tell you a claim is
uneconomic than run it for you.

**I want to sell but the co-owner refuses. What are my options?**
There are routes available, and which one applies depends on how the property is
held. Bring the title deed.
