## A contract is only worth what a UAE court will enforce

Most contract problems we are asked to litigate were created at the drafting stage —
usually by a template downloaded from the internet and written for another country's
legal system.

Online templates are typically drafted under English or US law. Key clauses —
governing law, dispute resolution, termination, penalties — are often unenforceable
or simply meaningless in the UAE. The document looks reassuring right up until the
moment you need it.

## Drafted in Arabic and English, together

UAE courts operate primarily in Arabic, and in a dispute the Arabic version is what
governs. We draft bilingually rather than writing in English and translating
afterwards — a translated contract is where meaning quietly changes.

## Common questions

**Are non-compete clauses enforceable in the UAE?**
Yes, but only if drafted correctly. UAE courts enforce non-competes that are
reasonable in scope — limited to a specific geographic area, a defined period, and a
genuine business interest. Overly broad clauses risk being struck down entirely,
which leaves you with no protection at all.

**Can you review a contract someone else has sent me?**
Yes. Review is often the highest-value work we do — it is far cheaper than the
dispute the clause would have caused.

**Do I really need the Arabic version?**
If you may ever need to enforce the contract in a UAE court, yes.
