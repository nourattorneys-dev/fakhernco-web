## Getting the structure right is the whole decision

Most company formation is sold as paperwork. It is not. The structure you choose
determines what you are allowed to trade, who can own what, how profits leave the
country, what happens if a founder leaves, and how easily you can sell or close
later. Changing it afterwards costs several times what getting it right costs at
the start.

We advise on the structure first, and handle the formation second.

## Which jurisdiction actually fits you

- **Mainland** — unrestricted access to the UAE market. You can trade directly
  with the local market, take on government and semi-government work, and open
  branches without a local distributor in between.
- **Free zone** — sector-focused hubs, each with its own regulator and its own
  rules on activity and ownership. DIFC and ADGM are the financial centres, with
  their own common-law frameworks and courts; there are options across Sharjah,
  RAK and the other Emirates at very different price points.
- **Offshore** — for holding structures, asset protection and international
  trade, rather than trading inside the UAE.

We will tell you honestly which your business needs, including the cases where
the cheapest licence is the wrong one — a free-zone company that cannot invoice
the mainland client it was set up to serve is a common and expensive mistake.

## What we do

- **Advise on jurisdiction, licence and activity**, so the licence actually
  covers what you will be invoicing for. Trading outside your licensed activity
  is a regulatory problem, not a technicality.
- **Draft the constitutional documents** — memorandum, articles, and the
  shareholder agreement that governs what happens when the founders disagree.
- **Handle registration end to end**, including name reservation, initial
  approvals, notarisation, attestation and licensing.
- **Set up governance** — board structure, signing authority, bank mandates and
  powers of attorney, so the company can actually operate once it exists.
- **Advise on the exit before you need it** — share transfers, restructuring, and
  the liquidation process, which is far easier to plan for than to improvise.

## Why a law firm rather than a formation agent

An agent files forms. The things that decide whether a company survives its first
disagreement are legal documents: a shareholder agreement that holds when two
founders fall out, a licence that matches the revenue, an ownership structure
that does not trap profits.

Those problems do not appear at formation. They appear two years later, usually
at the worst possible moment, and by then they are litigation rather than
drafting.

## The shareholder agreement is the document that matters

If you take one thing from this page: two-founder companies are the ones that
most often end up in dispute, and the shareholder agreement is what decides the
outcome when they do. It should answer, before there is any disagreement:

- Who decides what, and what needs unanimity
- What happens if one founder wants out, and how the shares are valued
- What happens on deadlock, death, or long-term incapacity
- Whether a departing founder can compete, and for how long
- How new investors come in, and on what terms

## Common questions

**Can I own 100% of my company?**
In many activities and jurisdictions, yes. It depends on the activity and where
you incorporate, which is exactly what we assess before recommending a structure.

**Mainland or free zone?**
It turns on who your customers are. If you need to invoice mainland UAE clients
directly, that points one way; if you are serving international clients from a
UAE base, it may point the other. It is worth an hour of advice before it is
worth a licence fee.

**Do I need a shareholder agreement if it is just me and one partner?**
Especially then.

**How long does formation take?**
It varies significantly by jurisdiction and activity. We give you a realistic
timeline with the recommendation rather than a best case.

**Can you handle contracts and employment documents once we are set up?**
Yes — contracts, employment agreements and ongoing corporate matters are handled
by the same team, which means the documents are consistent with the structure.
