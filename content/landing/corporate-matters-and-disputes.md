## When the dispute is inside the company

A shareholder dispute is not an ordinary commercial disagreement. The people on
the other side have access to the accounts, the staff, the bank mandate and the
customers. Every week it continues, the business is worth less — and unlike a
dispute with a supplier, both sides are destroying the same asset.

That is why speed matters here more than almost anywhere else in commercial law.

## Matters we handle

- Shareholder and partner disputes, including deadlock
- Removal or resignation of directors and managers
- Disputes over profit distribution, share valuation and transfers
- Breach of a shareholder or joint venture agreement
- Minority shareholder rights and oppression
- Misuse of company funds or assets
- Corporate restructuring, reorganisation and exit
- Liquidation, solvent and otherwise

## What we do

- **Establish your position first.** What the constitutional documents and the
  shareholder agreement actually entitle you to, before anyone escalates. A
  surprising number of disputes end at this stage, once one side sees the
  documents clearly.
- **Protect the company's assets** while the dispute runs — bank mandates,
  signing authority, access to books and records, and where necessary urgent
  applications to prevent dissipation.
- **Negotiate a structured exit.** A bought-out shareholder is almost always
  cheaper than a litigated one, and far quicker.
- **Litigate when it is necessary**, before the UAE courts or the relevant
  specialist forum.
- **Rebuild the governance** that allowed the dispute to happen, so it does not
  recur with the next partner.

## If you have been locked out

Being cut off from the accounts, the office or the company email is one of the
most common opening moves in a shareholder dispute, and one of the most
time-sensitive. There are steps available, and they work considerably better in
the first days than in the second month — partly because records can be altered,
and partly because a court takes a different view of a party who acted promptly.

Treat it as a same-week matter.

## Valuation is usually the real argument

Most shareholder disputes that look like arguments about principle are arguments
about price. Once both sides accept that someone is leaving, the dispute narrows
to what the departing share is worth and who pays for the valuation.

Getting to that question early, rather than after a year of hostility, is usually
worth more to a client than winning any individual procedural point.

Our strict non-conflict policy matters more in shareholder disputes than

other side is your business partner and may have spoken to firms in the same
market, that assurance is the basis of the whole relationship.

## Common questions

**My partner has locked me out of the company accounts. What can I do?**
There are urgent steps available and they are considerably more effective early.
Come in this week rather than next month.

**We have no shareholder agreement. Is there anything I can do?**
Yes. Your rights then come from the constitutional documents and UAE company law
rather than from a private agreement. That is a harder position, not a hopeless
one, and it is worth establishing precisely what it is.

**Can this be resolved without going to court?**
Often, and usually that is the better outcome. A negotiated exit preserves the
business's value; litigation between owners rarely does.

**The other side is also a friend or family member. Does that change anything?**
It changes the strategy, not the analysis. We will tell you what your position is
and let you decide how hard to press it.

**Can you act for the company rather than for me personally?**
Sometimes, but not always — the company and an individual shareholder can have
different interests, and we will be explicit at the outset about who our client
is.
