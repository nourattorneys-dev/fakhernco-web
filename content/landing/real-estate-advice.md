## The cheapest property dispute is the one you avoided

Almost every property dispute we litigate began with a document somebody signed
without advice — an off-plan agreement with no protection against delay, a lease
with an unenforceable renewal clause, a purchase where the title was never
properly checked.

This is advisory work, before the transaction. It costs a fraction of the
litigation it prevents.

## What we advise on

- **Purchase and sale** — reviewing the agreement before you sign, and advising
  on what it actually obliges each side to do
- **Off-plan purchases** — payment schedules, handover obligations, specification
  changes, and what happens if the developer is late
- **Title and due diligence** — ownership, encumbrances, mortgages, service
  charge arrears and restrictions on transfer
- **Leases** — commercial and residential, for landlords and tenants, including
  renewal, rent review and exit
- **Developer and construction agreements**
- **Ownership structures** — including holding property through a company, and
  what that means for transfer, financing and succession
- **Succession and inheritance** of UAE property, which does not work the way
  many owners assume

## Off-plan: what to negotiate before you sign

Off-plan purchases carry the risks that most reward advance advice:

1. **The completion date**, and what counts as an excusable extension.
2. **The remedy for delay** — whether you get compensation, a right to
   terminate, or nothing at all.
3. **The payment schedule**, and whether payments are tied to construction
   milestones or to dates.
4. **Specification changes** — how much the developer may alter, and what happens
   if the unit delivered is not the unit sold.
5. **Escrow** — whether your payments go where they are supposed to.

Developers will often say the contract is standard and cannot be changed.
Sometimes that is true, in which case the value of advice is knowing exactly what
you are accepting. Often it is not, and terms are negotiable — particularly on
delay and payment.

## Succession is the assumption that costs most

What happens to UAE property on the owner's death is one of the most common and
most expensive assumptions property owners make here. It does not necessarily
follow the law of your home country, and it does not necessarily follow the will
you made there.

There are structures and instruments that address it. All of them are easier to
put in place while you are buying than to unpick afterwards.

## Common questions

**Should a foreign buyer own property personally or through a company?**
It depends on the emirate, the area, your tax position and your succession plans.
It is worth deciding before you buy rather than restructuring afterwards.

**What happens to my UAE property if I die?**
Not necessarily what you expect. This is worth an hour of advice on its own.

**The agent says everyone signs this contract. Should I still have it reviewed?**
Yes. "Everyone signs it" is a statement about the agent's convenience, not about
your risk.

**Can you also act if it does become a dispute?**
Yes — the same team handles real estate and rental disputes, which means whoever
advised on the contract is the person who knows why the clause is there.
