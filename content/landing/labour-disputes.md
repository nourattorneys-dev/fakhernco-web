## Most labour disputes start at MOHRE, not in court

Most labour disputes in the UAE must first be filed with the Ministry of Human
Resources and Emiratisation — or with the relevant free zone authority — for
mediation. What happens at that stage shapes everything that follows, and it is
the stage people most often go into unrepresented, on the assumption that it is
merely administrative.

It is not. Positions taken at mediation are difficult to walk back later.

We act for both employers and employees, though never for both sides of the same
dispute.

## Matters we handle

- Unpaid salary, commission, overtime and end-of-service gratuity
- Termination, wrongful dismissal and arbitrary dismissal claims
- Labour bans — understanding them and, where possible, lifting them
- Disputes over contract terms, notice periods and non-compete clauses
- Free zone employment disputes, under the relevant zone's own authority
- Employer defence against claims, and getting the paperwork right beforehand
- Repatriation, visa cancellation and end-of-employment disputes

## What we do

- **Represent you at MOHRE mediation**, where most disputes are resolved.
- **Take it to the labour courts** when mediation does not settle it.

  can be done.
- **Enforce the award** — a decision in your favour is not the same as money in
  your account, and enforcement is a separate exercise.

## What to bring, and why it decides the case

Labour disputes are won on documents far more than on argument. Bring:

- The employment contract, including any earlier versions
- Any offer letter, which sometimes differs from the registered contract
- The termination or resignation letter
- Payslips, and WPS records if you have access to them
- Any written communication about salary, bonus or commission

The registered contract and the offer letter disagreeing with each other is one of
the most common situations we see, and which one governs is a question worth
answering before you file anything.

## For employers

The cheapest labour dispute is the one your contracts and procedures prevented.
We review employment contracts, termination processes and end-of-service
calculations so that a claim, if it comes, is one you can defend on paper.

Terminations handled without advice are the single most common source of
avoidable employer liability we see.

## Common questions

**Do I have to go to MOHRE before court?**
In most cases yes. MOHRE mediation, or the relevant free zone authority, is the
first stage and the courts expect it to have happened.

**I have been given a labour ban. Can it be lifted?**
Sometimes. It depends on why it was imposed and on your circumstances. We will
tell you at the consultation whether there is a realistic route rather than
taking the matter on regardless.

**I am still employed. Will my employer find out I have taken advice?**
A consultation is confidential, and nothing is filed without your instruction.

**How much gratuity am I entitled to?**
It depends on your contract type, your length of service and how the employment
ended. Bring the contract and we will calculate it with you rather than guess.

**My employer has not paid me for months. Should I resign first?**
Speak to us before you do. How and why an employment ends affects what you can
claim, and resigning at the wrong moment can weaken an otherwise strong position.
