## Most labour disputes start at MOHRE, not in court

Most labour disputes in the UAE must first be filed with the Ministry of Human
Resources and Emiratisation — or with the relevant free zone authority — for
mediation. What happens at that stage shapes everything that follows, and it is the
stage people most often go into unrepresented.

We act for both employers and employees, though never for both sides of the same
dispute.

## Matters we handle

- Unpaid salary, commission, overtime and end-of-service gratuity
- Termination, wrongful dismissal and arbitrary dismissal claims
- Labour bans — understanding them and, where possible, lifting them
- Disputes over contract terms, notice periods and non-compete clauses
- Free zone employment disputes, under the relevant zone's own authority
- Employer defence against claims, and getting the paperwork right beforehand

## What we do

- **Assess the claim honestly.** Whether you are owed what you think you are owed,
  and what the evidence actually supports.
- **Represent you at MOHRE mediation**, where most disputes are resolved.
- **Take it to the labour courts** when mediation does not settle it.
- **Deal with labour bans** imposed by MOHRE, and advise on what can realistically
  be done about them.
- **Enforce the award**, because a decision in your favour is not the same as money
  in your account.

## For employers

The cheapest labour dispute is the one your contracts and procedures prevented. We
review employment contracts, termination processes and end-of-service calculations
so that a claim, if it comes, is one you can defend.

## How it works

1. **A confidential consultation.** Bring the employment contract, any termination
   letter, and your payslips or WPS records if you have them.
2. **A clear assessment** of the merits, the likely outcome and the cost.
3. **We act** at MOHRE and, if necessary, before the labour courts.

## Common questions

**Do I have to go to MOHRE before court?**
In most cases yes — MOHRE mediation (or the relevant free zone authority) is the
first stage, and the courts expect it to have happened.

**I have been given a labour ban. Can it be lifted?**
Sometimes. It depends on why it was imposed and on your circumstances. We will tell
you at the consultation whether there is a realistic route, rather than taking the
matter on regardless.

**I am still employed. Will my employer find out I have taken advice?**
A consultation is confidential. Nothing is filed without your instruction.

**How much of my gratuity am I entitled to?**
It depends on your contract type, your length of service and how the employment
ended. Bring the contract and we will calculate it with you.
